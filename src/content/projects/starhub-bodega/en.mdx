## Context

StarHub is the tool a warehouse operator uses to record a container's arrival from their phone. They scan the receipt code, log every line they unload, note where each pallet goes and photograph damaged goods.

The service and the Android app were built by the team. My assignment was maintenance: fixing the defects that appeared once the system was already in production. On the app, which another colleague had started, I also connected the screens to the service's real data and added the timestamp seal on evidence photos.

## Problem

Goods-in was recorded on paper and someone transcribed it into the ERP later. Until then, inventory did not match what was in the warehouse. The discrepancy only appeared when someone went to fetch the goods and they were not where the system said.

Transcription also loses detail. Pallet positions and the condition of damaged goods rarely make it intact from paper to the system.

## Technical decisions

This service does not control the database schema. The tool the client runs its operation with defines it, and it can change without notice. All the maintenance work starts from that.

Database names are translated with SQL aliases in the data-access layer. They never reach the contract the Android app consumes. That contract therefore stays stable through a schema change, and the app version already tested in production keeps working untouched. The correspondence between columns is checked against the real data before it is written, because two columns of the same type are easy to mix up.

The warehouse queue filters by document type. The table it reads mixes receipts and dispatches. Without that filter, an operator would see outbound orders that look like receipts and try to receive cargo against one of them.

A field the database stops offering comes back null, not padded. If that removes functionality the operation was using, it is reported as such instead of being hidden in the mapping.

## Architecture

The service reads with direct SQL against the ERP's database and writes only through the ERP's API. The direct read is the fast path. Writes go through the API because it respects the sequence formulas and the triggers that a direct write would not fire. That separation already existed, and the maintenance work had to respect it.

A phone cannot authenticate with the platform's identity, because enrolment happens before the device holds any credential. Enrolment is therefore the service's control point, with four rules applied at once.

Once enrolled, the device works with a token scoped to its warehouse.

## Result

With StarHub, goods-in reaches the ERP at the moment the operator records it. It arrives with its lines, each pallet's position and the photos of damaged goods. Inventory stopped depending on a later transcription.

Schema changes in the ERP no longer require shipping a new version to the phones, and the queue only shows receipts.

One case was recorded as unverified, because checking it creates real rows in the ERP and requires the client's authorisation.

## What I learned

Against a platform another team manages, the name translation has to sit in the service's mapping layer, not in the mobile app's contract.

The other lesson is commercial. If a schema change removes something the operation was using, reporting it is part of the job. Resolving it silently leaves the people running the operation unaware that they lost something.

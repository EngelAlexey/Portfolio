## Context

The platform brings together Intercargo's public site and the portal its staff works in: client due diligence, accounts receivable, payroll, and tracking for the enquiries that arrive through the site. It is under active development and grows module by module.

The original brief was the internal portal. Rebuilding the public site as well was the team's proposal, because moving both onto infrastructure we control was the only way to run the portal with the guarantees the client asked for. I came in to build on the platform and to define its security model.

## Problem

The site ran on a third-party service the team did not administer. Without control over that infrastructure there was no way to host a portal holding client documents alongside it.

Due diligence was done by reading those documents by hand, file by file. Every new client took up a person's time before they could start operating, and the rate of new clients depended on how many people were free to review them.

## Technical decisions

A marketing site and a portal holding client documents have opposite exposure. Serving them from the same process would turn any fault in the first into access to the second.

The boundary went into the deployment rather than the code. A single image is published as two services with separate identities, and the public service has its database access removed.

The second rule is that server code is never imported from the browser, not even for types. A single constant imported from a server module pulled the whole model SDK along the dependency graph with it: hundreds of kilobytes of dead JavaScript on every visit. The rule is now checked by a command inside the pipeline.

## Architecture

The two services share an image and a binary. What they do not share is the identity they run as, and everything else follows from that.

A document uploaded by a client passes size, type and rate limits before it reaches the model. What the model returns is validated against the schema before it is written, so a malformed extraction never reaches the database.

## Result

The platform serves the public site, the internal portal, due diligence and accounts receivable from two services with different permissions. The document review that used to occupy a person is now proposed by the system, and the person confirms or corrects it.

Verification travels with every deployment: 2,243 unit tests across 144 files and 27 end-to-end suites, among them dedicated attack suites.

## What I learned

Splitting by deployment costs more than a check inside the code. It is two services to configure, two identities to maintain, and a deployment that can end up half-applied between versions.

In exchange, the guarantee stops depending on the application behaving as expected: the service that serves the public site holds no database credentials, and that stays true whatever happens above it. For a portal that holds client documents, that difference justifies the cost of running two services.

## Context

The platform brings together Intercargo's public site and the portal its staff works in. The portal covers client due diligence, accounts receivable, payroll and the tracking of the enquiries that arrive through the site. It is under active development and grows module by module.

The original brief was the internal portal. The team proposed rebuilding the public site as well. Moving both onto infrastructure the team controls was the only way to run the portal with the guarantees the client asked for. My role was to build on the platform and define its security model.

## Problem

The site ran on a third-party service the team did not administer. Without control over that infrastructure, there was no way to host a portal holding client documents alongside it.

Due diligence was done by reading those documents by hand, file by file. Every new client took up a person's time before they could start operating. The rate of new clients depended on how many people were free to review them.

## Technical decisions

A marketing site and a portal holding client documents have opposite exposure. Serving them from the same process would turn any fault in the first into access to the second.

So the boundary sits in the deployment, not in the code. The same image is published as two services with separate identities, and the public service has no access to the database.

The second rule is that browser code never imports server code, not even for types. A single constant imported from a server module pulled the whole model SDK into the browser's JavaScript through the dependency graph. That was hundreds of kilobytes of dead JavaScript on every visit. A command in continuous integration now checks that rule.

## Architecture

The two services share an image and a binary. Each one runs under its own identity, and all of its permissions depend on that identity.

A document uploaded by a client first passes the size, type and rate limits, and then reaches the model. What the model returns is validated against the schema before it is written, so a malformed extraction never reaches the database.

## Result

The platform serves the public site, the internal portal, due diligence and accounts receivable from two services with different permissions. The document review that used to occupy a person is now proposed by the system, and the person confirms or corrects it.

Every deployment goes through 2,243 unit tests across 144 files and 27 end-to-end suites, among them dedicated attack suites.

## What I learned

Splitting by deployment costs more than a check inside the code. It means two services to configure, two identities to maintain and a deployment that can end up half applied between versions.

In exchange, the guarantee does not depend on the application behaving as expected. The service behind the public site holds no database credentials, whatever its code does. For a portal that holds client documents, that guarantee justifies the cost of running two services.
